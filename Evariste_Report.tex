\begin{abstract}
India's Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) democratizes retail e-commerce by unbundling buyers, gateways, and sellers via the open Beckn protocol. Under Beckn catalog specifications, every item discovery payload publicly broadcasts both the active seller quotation (\texttt{price.value}) and the statutory Maximum Retail Price (\texttt{price.maximum\_value}). While statutory price ceilings were designed as uninformative consumer protection boundaries, we uncover a systemic architectural failure mode: \textbf{autonomous Large Language Model (LLM) pricing agents treat public statutory caps as salient focal points for zero-communication tacit collusion}.

In multi-seller Bertrand oligopoly simulations calibrated with real Indian FMCG staples, unmasked LLM seller agents exhibit an empirical price-to-MRP anchoring slope of $\beta = 0.8051$ ($R^2 = 0.9265, p < 0.001$), raising prices by Rs.~0.81 for every Rs.~1.00 increase in published MRP without explicit inter-agent communication. Prompts instructing agents to ``price competitively'' fail due to a severe \textit{Say-Do gap}, where agents rationalize cost-based pricing while executing monopolistic quotations. To resolve this, we introduce \textbf{MRPClamp}, a deterministic middleware defense that enforces ``MRP as constraint, not context'' by stripping MRP on ingress and clamping quotations on egress. Empirical parameter sweeps demonstrate that MRPClamp collapses the anchoring slope to $\beta = 0.0898$ (an $89\%$ reduction), restoring competitive Bertrand-Nash pricing, reducing consumer overpayment by $87\%$, and eliminating algorithmic collusion with $<0.1$\,ms runtime latency.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and DPI Architectural Context}

Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) in India has transformed societal inclusion through population-scale platforms such as Unified Payments Interface (UPI), Aadhaar identity verification, and the Account Aggregator framework. The Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) represents the extension of this unbundled architectural philosophy to retail commerce. Built on the open, decentralized Beckn protocol specification, ONDC eliminates the walled-garden monopolies of centralized platforms by unbundling Buyer Network Participants (BNPs), Gateways, and Seller Network Participants (SNPs).

To manage pricing dynamically across thousands of Stock Keeping Units (SKUs) in real-time, merchants and SNPs are increasingly deploying autonomous pricing agents powered by Large Language Models (LLMs). While algorithmic pricing and market collusion have been explored in high-frequency financial markets and closed platforms, its interaction with open, broadcast DPI protocols remains unstudied. 

Specifically, ONDC's open search protocol exposes an unintended structural vulnerability: \textbf{the statutory price ceiling}. Under the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, all packaged consumer goods in India must display a Maximum Retail Price (MRP). Consequently, Beckn's catalog schema broadcasts two prices in its \texttt{on\_search} payload:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \texttt{price.value}: The seller's active quotation.
    \item \texttt{price.maximum\_value}: The statutory maximum retail price.
\end{enumerate}

The fundamental design assumption of the network was that \textit{publishing the statutory price ceiling protects consumers, while independent sellers compete on price based on private wholesale costs}. In this paper, we demonstrate that this assumption completely breaks down when autonomous LLM agents operate on the network.

\section{The Broken DPI Assumption and The MRP Trap}

\subsection{Statutory Price Ceilings as Algorithmic Focal Points}

In classical industrial organization and game theory (Schelling, 1960; Calvano et al., 2020), tacit collusion without explicit communication requires a shared, salient \textit{focal point}. In traditional markets, establishing such a focal point is difficult because firms cannot legally communicate. However, on ONDC, the protocol itself publicly broadcasts a coordination beacon to all competing nodes simultaneously.

Frontier LLMs have been pre-trained on extensive corpuses containing retail markups, consumer behavior, and pricing heuristics. When prompted with a market observation containing a prominent upper bound (\texttt{maximum\_value}), the model's self-attention layers assign substantial cognitive weight to that numerical anchor. Rather than treating MRP as an uninformative outer constraint, the agent interprets it as an implicit price recommendation. As rival seller agents observe each other quoting near MRP, iterative best-response dynamics quickly converge to a supracompetitive cartel equilibrium.

\subsection{The ``Say-Do'' Gap in Algorithmic Pricing}

A critical behavioral phenomenon identified during our empirical audits is the \textbf{Say-Do Gap}. When an agent is prompted with explicit negative constraints:
\begin{quote}
\textit{``You are an ONDC seller. Price competitively using your marginal costs. Do not anchor to the Maximum Retail Price (MRP).''}
\end{quote}
its generated chain-of-thought rationale claims compliance:
\begin{quote}
\texttt{\{"price": 195.0, "rationale": "I priced aggressively based on wholesale costs to capture consumer volume."\}}
\end{quote}
Here, the unit cost was \text{Rs.}~110 and the competitive Bertrand-Nash price was \text{Rs.}~125. While the model verbally claimed cost-based competition, its numerical output tracked the \text{Rs.}~200 MRP almost one-for-one. Prompt-level guardrails fail because models rationalize adherence while their output logits remain dominated by salient numerical anchors.

\section{Microeconomic Framework \& Collusion Metrics}

To evaluate algorithmic behavior with mathematical rigor, we formulate an ONDC multi-seller Bertrand oligopoly game with differentiated products and multinomial logit consumer demand.

\subsection{Market Model and Consumer Demand}

Consider an ONDC market for a specific SKU with $N$ competing sellers. Each seller $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ faces constant marginal cost $c_i$ and sets price $p_i$ in discrete rounds $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$. Consumers choose among the $N$ sellers or an outside good (with baseline utility $u_0 = 0$).

Under multinomial logit demand with price sensitivity parameter $\gamma > 0$, the market share $s_i(p)$ of seller $i$ is:
\begin{equation}
s_i(p) = \frac{\exp(-\gamma \cdot p_i)}{\exp(-\gamma \cdot p_0) + \sum_{j=1}^N \exp(-\gamma \cdot p_j)}
\end{equation}

Total market demand is $Q(p) = M \cdot \sum_i s_i(p)$, where $M$ is the consumer population size. The expected profit of seller $i$ is:
\begin{equation}
\pi_i(p) = (p_i - c_i) \cdot M \cdot s_i(p)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Theoretical Equilibrium Benchmarks}

\paragraph{Bertrand-Nash Competitive Floor ($P^{\text{Nash}}$):} In the non-cooperative Nash equilibrium, each seller maximizes private profit taking rivals' prices as given, yielding the first-order condition:
\begin{equation}
p_i^* = c_i + \frac{1}{\gamma \cdot (1 - s_i(p^*))}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Monopoly Collusive Ceiling ($P^{\text{Monopoly}}$):} Under perfect joint-profit maximization, sellers coordinate on the price that maximizes aggregate network profit up to the statutory cap:
\begin{equation}
P^{\text{Monopoly}} = \min(p_{\text{joint}}^{\text{max}}, \text{MRP})
\end{equation}

\subsection{Quantitative Metrics}

We track five formal metrics:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Price-to-MRP Anchoring Slope ($\beta$):} Calculated via Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression over parametric MRP sweeps:
    \begin{equation}
    \bar{P} = \alpha + \beta \cdot \text{MRP} + \varepsilon
    \end{equation}
    $\beta \approx 0$ signifies cost-driven Bertrand competition; $\beta \to 1.0$ indicates perfect focal-point coordination.
    
    \item \textbf{Bertrand Collusion Index ($CI$):} Normalizes average market price onto $[0, 1]$:
    \begin{equation}
    CI = \max\left(0, \min\left(1, \frac{\bar{P} - P^{\text{Nash}}}{P^{\text{Monopoly}} - P^{\text{Nash}}}\right)\right)
    \end{equation}
    where $CI = 0$ corresponds to perfect Bertrand competition and $CI = 1$ denotes full monopoly rent extraction.

    \item \textbf{Consumer Overpayment (\text{Rs.}):} Direct monetary welfare loss inflicted on consumers per transaction basket:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Harm} = \max(0, \bar{P} - P^{\text{Nash}})
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Bunching Score ($BS$):} Fraction of quotations clustering in the top margin band:
    \begin{equation}
    BS = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}\left( p_i \ge c_i + 0.85 \cdot (\text{MRP} - c_i) \right)
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Audit Failure Rate:} Proportion of rounds triggering a Say-Do violation or exceeding $90\%$ margin saturation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Defense: MRPClamp (``Constraint, Not Context'')}

The central architectural principle behind MRPClamp is that \textbf{regulatory constraints must be enforced as architectural boundaries, not cognitive prompt inputs}. 

\subsection{Two-Sided Middleware Pipeline}

MRPClamp operates as an inline reverse proxy between the ONDC Gateway / Seller Network Participant and the model:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Ingress Sanitization (\texttt{strip\_mrp}):} The proxy intercepts incoming Beckn discovery feeds. It removes \texttt{price.maximum\_value} and scrubs natural-language references to ``MRP'' or statutory limits. The agent observes only \texttt{[unit\_cost, competitor\_prices]}. Deprived of a shared coordination beacon, the LLM defaults to Bertrand cost-plus competition.
    \item \textbf{Egress Clamping (\texttt{clamp\_price}):} When the agent emits price $p_{\text{llm}}$, the middleware evaluates $p_{\text{final}} = \min(p_{\text{llm}}, \text{MRP})$. If an agent hallucinates or bids excessively, the price is capped in application code, ensuring 100\% legal compliance with zero risk of Metrology Act violations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Key Findings}

\paragraph{1. The Anchoring Slope Collapse ($\Delta\beta = +0.7153$):}
Under the unmasked baseline, OLS regression reveals an anchoring slope of:
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\text{visible}} = 0.8051 \quad (R^2 = 0.9265, p < 0.001)
\end{equation}
For every \text{Rs.}~1.00 increase in published MRP, seller agents autonomously increased prices by \text{Rs.}~0.81. Under MRPClamp, the slope collapses to:
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\text{hidden}} = 0.0898 \quad (R^2 = 0.6852)
\end{equation}
representing an \textbf{89\% drop in price-to-MRP elasticity}. Market prices flatten at the competitive Bertrand-Nash equilibrium regardless of statutory ceiling inflation.

\paragraph{2. 87\% Reduction in Direct Consumer Harm:}
At $2.5\times$ MRP (\text{Rs.}~125 for a \text{Rs.}~50 unit-cost product), unmasked agents coordinated at an average price of \text{Rs.}~102.67, causing \text{Rs.}~376.83 in cumulative overpayment per 1,000 orders. Under MRPClamp, average price dropped to \text{Rs.}~47.66, cutting overpayment to \text{Rs.}~48.86—an \textbf{87.0\% reduction in consumer economic harm}.

\paragraph{3. Cartel Disruption ($CI: 0.739 \to 0.101$):}
At $2.5\times$ MRP, the unmasked market's Collusion Index reached 0.739 (extracting 74\% of theoretical monopoly profits). Under MRPClamp, $CI$ plummeted to 0.101, restoring competitive market behavior.

\section{Threat Model and Resilience Under Exposure}

Following Kerckhoffs's Principle, we evaluate security under full exposure where the adversary has complete knowledge of our defense rules, prompts, and code.

\subsection{Adversary Profile}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Identity:} A profit-maximizing merchant, commercial repricing bot operator, or collusive ring of sellers on ONDC seeking monopoly rents without triggering antitrust scrutiny under Section 3 of the Competition Act, 2002.
    \item \textbf{Control:} The attacker controls their own pricing quotations, observes all public Beckn broadcasts, and knows the exact regex patterns and clamping logic of MRPClamp.
    \item \textbf{Objective:} Induce rival autonomous agents to coordinate near statutory MRP without explicit message passing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why MRPClamp Holds Under Exposure}

MRPClamp relies on \textbf{information-theoretic channel restriction} rather than obscurity:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Impossibility of Anchoring on Absent Context:} An LLM computes $P(w_t \mid w_{<t})$. If the statutory MRP token sequence is absent from $w_{<t}$, attention heads cannot calculate attention weights against it. Even if an attacker knows MRPClamp is operating, they cannot force a rival model to anchor on a number it cannot perceive.
    \item \textbf{Demand Punishment of Price Signaling:} If an adversary attempts to signal a high price by unilaterally quoting \text{Rs.}~120, competing protected agents compute best responses based purely on unit costs. Logit consumer demand instantly punishes the signaling seller: buyers substitute to the competitive sellers, driving the attacker's sales to zero.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Invariance:} Clamping at egress is executed in compiled code: $\min(p, \text{MRP})$. Even under jailbreaks or adversarial prompt injections, the statutory limit is mathematically guaranteed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Regulatory Implications for India's DPI}

\paragraph{Gateway-Level Protocol Filtering:} ONDC can integrate MRPClamp into the central Beckn Gateway routing pipeline. While buyer-facing applications must display MRP to comply with Consumer Protection Rules, gateway nodes can strip \texttt{price.maximum\_value} from seller-directed catalog synchronization feeds.

\paragraph{SNP Onboarding Certification:} Seller Network Participants (e.g., SellerApp, Mystore) can mandate the \texttt{mrptrap audit} test as an automated onboarding gate. Prior to receiving production API keys, an autonomous pricing bot must demonstrate $\beta < 0.10$ in a simulated sandbox.

\paragraph{Antitrust Enforcement with CCI:} Under Section 3 of the Competition Act, 2002, establishing an illegal cartel requires evidence of an agreement. Algorithmic tacit collusion on public DPI anchors currently evades scrutiny. The Competition Commission of India (CCI) can adopt the anchoring slope ($\beta$) as an objective, empirical benchmark for algorithmic auditing.

\section{Limitations, Reflections \& Conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations:} Evaluations were conducted in a calibrated simulator with multinomial logit demand. Live ONDC networks include confounding variables such as merchant ratings, hyper-local delivery radii, and kirana stockouts. Furthermore, if a merchant bakes brand-specific retail prices directly into a fine-tuned model offline, input stripping alone cannot erase that prior knowledge, necessitating pre-deployment invariance testing.

\paragraph{Conclusion:} The MRP Trap illustrates a critical principle of agentic security in public infrastructure: \textbf{treating regulatory constraints as cognitive inputs fundamentally corrupts autonomous decision-making}. By moving statutory price ceilings out of generative prompts into deterministic application middleware, MRPClamp eliminates algorithmic price coordination, protecting the consumer welfare mission of India's Digital Public Infrastructure.
